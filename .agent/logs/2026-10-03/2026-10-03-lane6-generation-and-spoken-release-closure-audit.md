## Spoken accessibility: actual requirement and available capability

[Accessibility guidance](../../../docs/ACCESSIBILITY.md) requires current
Chrome with NVDA or Windows Narrator, keyboard only, on the packaged candidate.
Record browser/AT version, viewport, date, tester and findings. It explicitly
says automated checks and accessibility-tree inspection do not establish
spoken announcements.

For the changed Lane 6 flows, the concrete scenarios are:

- Smaller-target pending count after reload; named modal and range; exact-count
  confirmation; same-identity uncertain retry; focus return; separate additional
  cost/unknown previous cost warning before any paid retry; mobile overflow.
- Ask disclosure before submit; distinct related/no-match/clarification/
  temporary-failure/withdrawn/fallback states; unverified reading warning and
  document/page/section/exact quote announcement order; paid retry dialog.
- Original-PDF opening by keyboard; dialog focus/loading/page number and
  previous/next controls; selectable text and extracted alternative; Escape
  and focus return; missing original, stale/cancelled/access-revoked hiding;
  mobile wrapping.
- Disabled-Ask published lecture search/browse, names/results and keyboard
  original/text opening; revoke publication/enrollment while open, remove stale
  results, close dialog and restore focus to the lecture area; mobile width.

The guide also lists authentication, student set/progress, untimed/timed Study,
failed-save retry and ordinary dialogs. These remain required when the packaged
release includes their changed behavior; prior automated checks do not supply
the spoken evidence. They are not waived by this bounded audit.

Available supported browser tooling can inspect rendered UI/AX, operate the
keyboard and capture screenshots. Native-computer APIs are disabled in this
host capability, and the tool inventory supplies no screen-reader speech or
audio capture. It therefore cannot independently prove the NVDA/Narrator
announcement pass. A tester must perform and record actual spoken observations,
or a supported speech-observation capability must be provided. Launching an
AT executable or synthesizing speech from accessible names would not prove
that the screen reader actually announced the app correctly.
